- **CFG-35** A MySQL catalog runs its sessions on one connection in every runtime: a session whose transaction ends with an acknowledged `COMMIT` or `ROLLBACK` leaves its connection to the next session. A catalog read that its budget stops (INT-115) returns without reading the rest of the result, and ends its statement with `KILL QUERY` of the id of its connection, which `SELECT CONNECTION_ID()` answers, on a second connection, the kill connection, which the catalog opens at its first stop and keeps until it closes. Through ProxySQL that id is the id of the ProxySQL session, which ProxySQL answers for that statement and which its `KILL QUERY` names. The stopped connection then reads the end of the killed statement, rolls back and stays, so a catalog whose stops keep their connections opens no other connection, and its user needs two places in `max_user_connections`. When the server refuses the kill connection or the kill fails, the read closes its socket instead, with no wait, as a PostgreSQL read that its budget stops does. Under ProxySQL's `mysql-kill_backend_connection_when_disconnect`, `true` by default, the close then ends at once a statement that sends nothing, such as a long scan or `SLEEP`, which runs to its end under `false`. A connection that the catalog closes holds its place in `max_user_connections` until the server ends that thread, which the server announces to no one, so a connect of that catalog that the limit refuses within 2 s of that close is tried again until those 2 s end (a timer, for want of any notice; within 40 ms measured). A connect that follows no close of that catalog fails at once. A budget stop that closed the connection attempts no rollback, and its error is `CATALOG_LIMIT` alone. The case `budget_stops_keep_the_connections_of_the_catalog` of the catalog recipe stops two reads and reads twice as a user of two places, whom the server admits two connections for.
